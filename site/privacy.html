Privacy policy

JustNow records your screen so you can rewind it. Everything it records stays on your Mac. There is no account, no telemetry, and no upload of screen content, recognised text, or settings. The only network requests the app makes are update checks.

Effective date: [EFFECTIVE DATE]. Applies to JustNow for macOS as distributed from GitHub Releases and this website.

What JustNow captures

JustNow only captures your screen after you grant it Screen Recording permission in macOS. From then on, while JustNow is running and recording is not paused, it takes a still image of each connected display at the capture interval you choose in Settings.

Those images contain whatever was visible on screen. That can include messages, documents, private browsing windows, and passwords or codes that were shown in plain text. JustNow does not try to detect or skip sensitive content, so pause recording whenever you would rather it did not keep a copy.

JustNow stops capturing while the screen is locked, while the Mac is asleep, and while another user is logged in through fast user switching.

Pausing recording

You can pause and resume recording at any time with ⌘⌥⇧J (configurable in Settings) or with Pause Recording in the menu bar menu. Quitting JustNow also stops capture. To stop JustNow capturing entirely, quit it and turn it off in System Settings → Privacy & Security → Screen Recording.

Where your history is stored

Captured frames are saved as JPEG images, with a local SQLite database of frame details (such as capture times and which display they came from), in ~/Library/Application Support/JustNow/. JustNow restricts that folder to your macOS user account and excludes it from Time Machine and other backups. JustNow does not add its own encryption to these files, so we recommend turning on FileVault.

If you turn on Keep recent history in memory in Settings, the most recent detail is held in memory instead of being written to disk, and is cleared when JustNow quits or relaunches.

If JustNow finds damaged or incomplete storage when it starts, it moves the affected files into a recovery folder inside the same storage folder instead of deleting them. Those copies stay there until you clear your history or delete them yourself.

How long history is kept

You choose how much rewind history to keep: 30 minutes, 2 hours, 8 hours, or 24 hours (the default). JustNow keeps every frame for the most recent part of that window, then keeps fewer frames as history gets older. Frames older than the window are deleted automatically, along with their recognised text. When the window is shorter than one hour, JustNow keeps a sparse hour of history so that the one-hour search option still works.

Recognised text and search

To make your history searchable, JustNow runs text recognition on captured frames using Apple’s Vision framework, on your Mac. The recognised text and the position of each word are stored in a local search database in the same storage folder. Neither the images nor the text are sent anywhere. Recognised text is deleted when its frame is deleted.

Deleting your history

In Settings → Capture → Storage, choose Clear All History. This deletes all captured frames, the recognised-text search database, and any recovery copies. Open Storage Folder in the same section shows you the files. You can also quit JustNow and delete ~/Library/Application Support/JustNow/ yourself.

As with any file deletion, macOS may not immediately overwrite the disk space that deleted data occupied. FileVault protects that space as well.

Clipboard and saved screenshots

JustNow only copies or saves something when you ask it to:

  • Dragging over text in a rewind frame copies the recognised text to your clipboard.
  • Saving a frame or region (⌘S, or holding ⌘ while dragging) writes a JPEG to the folder you choose in Settings (your macOS screenshot location by default), copies it to the clipboard, or both.

Once something is on your clipboard or in a folder, it is handled by macOS and any other apps you use. For example, Universal Clipboard or iCloud Drive syncing of your Desktop may copy it to your other devices. JustNow does not track or delete those copies.

Diagnostics log

To help debug capture problems, JustNow keeps a small diagnostics log at ~/Library/Logs/JustNow/. It records capture lifecycle events (starts, stops, errors, permission checks, and sleep or lock transitions) and occasional aggregate measurements such as frame counts, total data sizes, and queue lengths.

The log never contains screen content, recognised text, image data, file paths, display names or identifiers, or perceptual hashes (image fingerprints). It is capped at about 1 MB, is never sent anywhere by JustNow, and you can delete it at any time. If you attach it to a bug report, you choose what to share.

Network requests and updates

The only network requests JustNow makes are for software updates, using the open-source Sparkle framework:

  • Checking for updates downloads the public update feed from https://justnow.tk.sg/appcast.xml, which is hosted on Cloudflare Pages.
  • If an update is available and you choose to install it (or turn on automatic downloads), JustNow downloads the signed update from GitHub Releases.

These requests carry the details any web request does, such as your IP address, plus the app and Sparkle versions. JustNow does not send a system profile, a device identifier, or anything about your history. Cloudflare and GitHub may keep standard server logs under their own privacy policies. You can turn off automatic checks in Settings → General → Software Update and check manually instead.

What JustNow does not do

  • No analytics, telemetry, or crash reporting services.
  • No account or sign-in.
  • No upload or cloud sync of screen history, recognised text, or settings.
  • No advertising, and no selling or sharing of data, because we never receive any.
  • No use of your data to train AI models.

The Search Diagnostics figures in Settings are calculated and shown on your Mac only.

This website

justnow.tk.sg is a static site. It sets no cookies and runs no analytics or tracking scripts. Cloudflare, which hosts it, may process standard request logs.
